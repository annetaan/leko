Overlapping holes — clip-path: path(evenodd) vs an SVG mask

Leko cuts its holes with one clip-path: path(evenodd, …): an outer rectangle and one rounded-rectangle subpath per hole. Even-odd counts crossings, so a point inside two holes is inside an even number of subpaths and paints dark. That is not a detail — it means two holes may never overlap, and an opening where each hole converges inward from off the surface is exactly a set of holes that overlap while they travel.

An SVG mask has no parity: it paints white for what shows and black for what is cut, and black over black is still black. The question is what that costs. Leko's scrim is as large as the scrollable area, which is thousands of pixels tall, and a mask may allocate a surface that size where a clip path allocates nothing.

M1 — two holes that overlap

The same two rounded rectangles, given to each technique. Judge by eye: on the left, the region where they overlap should be dark, which is the bug; on the right it should be one bright hole. Then play the opening — both holes start as the whole stage and converge, the way a canvas source-out overlay does it — and watch what happens in between.

M2 / M3 — a surface as tall as a document

Each scroller holds 12 000px of content with a scrim over all of it, which is the shape Leko's own scrim takes on a long page. M2 animates three holes for 120 frames on each and reports the frame times — that verdict is the page's own. M3 is by eye: scroll to the bottom, or use the button, and check that the hole 11 500px down is still cut. A mask that quietly stops applying past some surface size would show as a scrim with no hole in it down there.

M6 / M7 — the two routes that do not reference a mask from CSS

M5 says an engine may refuse to reach an SVG <mask> from CSS. Neither of these asks it to. M6 builds the mask out of CSS layers instead: one opaque layer for the surface, one image layer per hole, and mask-composite to take the holes out of the surface — the holes union with add, so overlap costs nothing here either. Each hole layer is a rounded rectangle in an SVG carried as a data: URL, which is an ordinary mask image rather than a reference to a document node. M7 does not use CSS masking at all: the scrim becomes an <svg>, the dimming becomes a <rect> inside it, and the mask is applied to that rect with the SVG mask attribute — SVG masking SVG, which is the oldest of these features.

M5 — which spelling of the mask a browser honours

The same white-background-and-black-holes mask, referenced eight ways. Every panel should show two bright holes joined into one. By eye: a panel that is uniformly dark is a mask that did nothing — the reference was taken as an alpha mask, where an opaque white rectangle and an opaque black one are equally opaque and nothing is cut. A panel with no dimming at all is a mask that resolved to nothing, and hid the scrim entirely.

M4 — does a mask take the scrim out of hit-testing, the way a clip does

It should not, and that is the answer wanted here rather than feared: Leko's scrim already carries pointer-events: none and does its blocking with rectangles beside it, precisely because a clip path takes an element out of hit-testing but not out of the search for what a wheel should scroll (../wheel-through-a-hole/). If a mask changes nothing about hit-testing, nothing about the blocking has to be reconsidered. The scrims here are given pointer-events: auto for the length of the probe, so that there is something to answer.
