Can a fixed scrim cover the document scrollbar's gutter?

A position: fixed element stops at the layout viewport, which is documentElement.clientWidth — the scrollbar excluded. Sizing a scrim to that is right at the moment it is measured and wrong as soon as the scrollbar comes or goes, and a scrim that is then too narrow leaves a strip of the page uncovered and unblocked. window.innerWidth covers the gutter and does not move when the scrollbar does, and this page measures what that costs.

Three questions. Whether a fixed box wider than the layout viewport adds scrollable overflow, which would put a scrollbar on every page a tour runs on. Whether the document scrollbar still paints over such a box, which decides whether covering the gutter is invisible or a visible regression. And what notices the scrollbar coming and going, since a resize event does not.

1. Does a fixed box wider than the layout viewport grow the page?

A fixed-position box is meant to be left out of the document's scrollable overflow region, so a scrim wider than clientWidth should not grow a scrollbar out of the page it is dimming. Deliberately asked with several widths rather than only innerWidth: on a platform with overlay scrollbars innerWidth is clientWidth and a pass would prove nothing, while a box 40px or 400px over is the same question with the platform taken out of it. The height is over by the same amount, because the layer is sized on both axes.

2. Does the scrollbar still paint over it?

After the measurement the gutter column is left filled in solid red — from documentElement.clientWidth to window.innerWidth, which is exactly the strip a fixed box at clientWidth leaves out. If the scrollbar is drawn on top of the red, a scrim covering the gutter is invisible. If the red is drawn on top of the scrollbar, a scrim covering the gutter dims the scrollbar, and that is a regression rather than a fix. This one is a call by eye, on a classic-scrollbar platform: nothing in the DOM reports what painted last, a screenshot taken over the DevTools protocol does not reliably carry the scrollbar, and where the scrollbars are overlay there is no gutter and nothing is drawn at all. The banner above says which kind this browser has.

3. What notices the scrollbar coming and going?

The page is made tall, then short, then tall again, and each transition is watched. A resize event fires when the window is resized, which this is not. A ResizeObserver on documentElement fires when its content box changes, which a page growing or shrinking does — and on a classic-scrollbar platform the scrollbar leaving is part of that same change. The clientWidth column is the gutter appearing and disappearing, and is the bug this is all about; where it does not move, the resize and observer counts still stand, because they are counted against a change to the root's box either way.
