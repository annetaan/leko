How long does a smooth scroll take, and what says when it is over?

A tour that scrolls to its target has two animations to think about: the scroll, and the morph that carries the hole to where the target ended up. Running them together is only safe if everything the morph is drawn from is the same at the start of the scroll as at the end of it — and what is not is every decision made in viewport coordinates: which side of the hole the message takes, and which corner the way out sits in. Both are chosen once, from where the hole is on screen at that moment.

So the question is how far off that moment can be, and what an implementation can wait for instead. The fourth question was not planned: a nested scroller turned out to answer the first one differently depending on whether it was on screen, and the sweep is what that became. The fifth is what a tour that moves on mid-glide is allowed to do about the glide.

1 — How long a smooth scroll runs, and where it is at 320ms

Four distances on the document and one inside a nested scroller. Each is driven from a standing start, sampled every frame until the offset stops changing, and reported as: how long it took to settle, how many pixels were still to go at 320ms — the default length of a Leko morph — and what fraction of the trip that is. The pixels-still-to-go column is the whole question: it is how far the page moves under a decision made at that instant.

4 — Does a scroller below the fold scroll at all?

The same 1200px scroll inside the same panel, with only one thing changed: how far down the page the panel sits, the window left at the top. A panel a tour is about to scroll is below the fold by definition — the page has not moved yet — so this is not an edge case, it is the ordinary one. 5 — Can a glide be stopped, or replaced, part-way?

A tour that moves on while the page is still gliding has two things it might do to the glide. Set the page to where it is at that instant — CSSOM View says a scroll of any kind aborts the smooth scroll in flight, so an instant one to the current offset ought to leave the page there. Or start a second smooth scroll to a destination of its own and have it land there rather than fight the first — asked two ways, as a scrollBy by the delta from where scrollY says the page is, and as a scrollTo of the same offset, because those ought to be the same request. All three are asked 100ms into a 5000px glide, which is far enough in that every engine is moving and short enough that none has arrived.
